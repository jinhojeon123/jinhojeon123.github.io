# Fatou's Lemma

## Statement

If $\{  f_n \}$ iis any sequence in $L^{+}$, then 
$$
    \int \liminf f_n  \le {\liminf \int f_n }
$$ 
## Proof

<!-- how to build f1 <= f2 <= f3 <= ...  -->

Let $ 
g_n(x)=\inf_{k\ge n} f_k(x).
\ $   


for all $ n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}, \\ $

$$
    0 \le g_n \le g_{n+1}  
$$

By MCT, 
$$
    \int { \lim g_n} = {\lim} \int g_n
$$



On the left side, 

$$
    \int {\liminf f_n }
$$

On the right side,

$$
    \lim \int g_n
$$

However, 

$$
    g_n \le f_k \quad \forall k \ge n
$$

By Monotonicity, 

$$
    \int g_n \le \int f_k 
$$

and by inf 

$$
    \int g_n \le \inf_{k\ge n} \int f_k  \quad (\because \ g_n \le \inf_{k\ge n} f_k)
$$


and by lim 

$$
    \lim_{n\to \infty} \int g_n \le \lim_{n \to \infty} \inf_{k\ge n} \int f_k 
$$

Then by definition of $\liminf$, 

$$
        \lim_{n \to \infty} \inf_{k \ge n} a_k = \liminf_{n \to \infty} a_n
$$


Hence, 

$$
    \lim_{n\to \infty} \int g_n \le \lim_{n \to \infty} \inf_{k\ge n} \int f_k = \liminf_{n\to \infty} \int f_n 
$$

Since, 

$$ 
    \int \liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int g_n 
$$

Therefore, 

$$
\int \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n.
\qquad\square
$$

# Dominated Convergence Theorem

## Statement

Let $\{ f_n \}$ be a sequence in $L^{1}$ such that 
$$
    \begin{aligned}
        (a)\:& f_n \to f \quad \text{a.e.},\\
        (b)\:& \exists g \in L^{1} \quad s.t \ |f_n| \le g \ \text{a.e.} \ \forall n. 
    \end{aligned}
$$

Then $f\in L^{1}$ and $\int f = \lim_{n \to \infty}  \int f_n$ 

## Proof


<!-- f \in L^1 -->
<!-- Claim : ? |f| < \infty > -->

Since $\forall n$, $f_n \le g$, 

$$

    |f| = \lim_{n \to \infty} |f_n| \le g
$$

Therefore, 

$$
    \int |f| \le \int |g| < \infty \quad (\because g \in L^{1})
$$

Hence, $f \in L^{1}$

Since $|f_n| \le g$, 

$$
        -g \le f_n \le g \quad \forall n
$$

and 
 
$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &\le f_n + g - (1) ,\\
0 &\le g - f_n - (2).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\{ f_n + g\}$ and $\{g-f_n\}$ are included in $L^{+}$ and $\lim {f_n + g} = f + g$, $\lim{g- {f_n}} = g -f$,  w

we can use Fatou Lemma for each inequalities, Then:

$$
    \int \liminf (g + f_n) \le \liminf \int {g + f_n } - (3)
$$

and 

$$
    \int \liminf (g - f_n) \le \liminf \int {g - f_n } - (4)
$$


in inequality (4), 

$$
    \int \liminf (g - f_n) \le \liminf \int {g - f_n } - (4)
$$

$\leftrightarrow$

$$
    \int \liminf (g - f_n) \le \liminf \int {g - f_n } = {\liminf} \ {(\int g - \int f_n)}- (4)
$$

Because $ \liminf_{n \to \infty} {C + f_n} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \inf_{k\ge n} {C + f_k}= \lim_{n \to \infty} (C + \inf_{k\ge} f_k) = C + \liminf f_n$,

inequality (4) becomes as below:

$$
    \int \liminf (g- f_n) \le \liminf \int {g - f_n } = {\liminf} \ {(\int g - \int f_n)}- (5)
$$

and Since

$$
    {\liminf} \ {(\int g - \int f_n)} 
    = { \int g } + {\liminf (-\int f_n)} = {\int g} - \limsup {\int f} \quad (\because \liminf (-a_n) = - \limsup a_n)
$$

and 

$$
    \int {\liminf (g-f_n)} = \int g = \int g - \int f
$$

inequality (5) becomes 

$$
        \int g - \int f \le \int g - \limsup \int f_n
$$

$\Leftrightarrow$

$$
         \int f \ge \limsup \int f_n - (6)
$$

Likewise, From $g + f_n \ge 0 $, we get

$$
        \int f \le  \liminf  \int f_n - (7)
$$

By (6), (7), we get:

$$
       \limsup \int f_n  \le \int f \le \liminf \int f_n 
$$

Since $\limsup \int f_n =  \liminf \int f_n $

$$
        \lim_{n \to \infty} \int f_n = \int f. \quad  \square
$$
