# Fatou's Lemma

## Statement

For a sequence of nonnegative measurable functions $\{f_n\}\subset L^+$,

$$
\int\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_n
\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n.
$$

- Limitation of the current explanation: The common measure space and the notation $L^+$ are omitted.
- Suggested addition: Specify $(X,\mathcal M,\mu)$, the extended-valued codomain of $L^+$, and the integral notation at the beginning of the note.
- Reason: Nonnegative integrals that may be infinite must be distinguished from the finite $L^1$ integrals used later.

## Proof

Define the tail infimum by

$$
g_n(x)=\inf_{k\ge n}f_k(x)
$$

Then $0\le g_n\le g_{n+1}$, each $g_n$ is measurable, and $g_n\uparrow\liminf f_n$. MCT gives

$$
\int\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_n
=\int\lim_{n\to\infty}g_n
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int g_n.
$$

Since $g_n\le f_k$ for every $k\ge n$, monotonicity of the integral gives

$$
\int g_n\le\int f_k\quad(k\ge n),
\qquad
\int g_n\le\inf_{k\ge n}\int f_k.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\int\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_n
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int g_n
\le\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{k\ge n}\int f_k
=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n.
\qquad\square
$$

## Proof idea / Assumptions

The tail infima increase even when the original sequence is neither convergent nor monotone. Applying MCT to this sequence is the key step. Nonnegativity and measurability remain essential, and the inequality may be strict.

# Dominated Convergence Theorem

## Statement

Suppose a sequence of measurable functions $\{f_n\}$ satisfies $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere, and there is some $g\in L^1$ such that $|f_n|\le g$ almost everywhere for every $n$. Then $f\in L^1$ and

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n=\int f.
$$

- Issue: Measurability of the limit $f$ and the scalar field of the functions are unspecified.
- Why this matters: On a noncomplete measure space, an arbitrary representative of an almost-everywhere limit need not be measurable. The proof below using $-g\le f_n\le g$ applies directly only to real-valued functions.
- Required revision: Either assume that $f$ is measurable or choose a measurable representative. State that the theorem is real-valued; to include a complex-valued version, add the reduction to real and imaginary parts. Handle almost-everywhere conditions outside a common null set.

## Proof

From $|f_n|\to|f|$ almost everywhere and $|f_n|\le g$, we obtain $|f|\le g$ almost everywhere. Therefore,

$$
\int|f|\le\int g<\infty,
\qquad f\in L^1.
$$

Since $-g\le f_n\le g$, apply Fatou's Lemma to $g+f_n$ and $g-f_n$.

### Step 1: Fatou for $g+f_n$

Since $g+f_n\to g+f$ almost everywhere,

$$
\int(g+f)\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int(g+f_n).
$$

Because $g\in L^1$, the finite constant $\int g$ can be separated and subtracted.

$$
\int g+\int f
\le\int g+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n,
\qquad
\int f\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n.
\tag{1}
$$

### Step 2: Fatou for $g-f_n$

Since $g-f_n\to g-f$ almost everywhere,

$$
\int(g-f)\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int(g-f_n),
$$

$$
\int g-\int f
\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\int g-\int f_n\right).
$$

Using $\liminf(C-a_n)=C-\limsup a_n$ gives

$$
\int g-\int f\le\int g-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int f_n,
\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int f_n\le\int f.
\tag{2}
$$

From (1), (2), and $\liminf a_n\le\limsup a_n$,

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int f_n
\le\int f
\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int f_n
\le\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int f_n.
$$

Thus $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n=\int f$. $\square$

## Why Domination Is Necessary

On $(0,1)$, let

$$
f_n(x)=n\mathbf1_{(0,1/n)}(x)
$$

Then $f_n(x)\to0$ for every $x>0$, but

$$
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx=1\quad\text{for every }n.
$$

Consequently, $\lim_n\int f_n=1\ne0=\int\lim_n f_n$. Integrable domination prevents this kind of mass concentration.

## Comparison / What I should remember

| Result | Assumptions | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| MCT | $0\le f_n\uparrow f$, measurable | $\int f_n\to\int f$ |
| Fatou | $f_n\ge0$, measurable | $\int\liminf f_n\le\liminf\int f_n$ |
| DCT | measurable $f_n,f$, $f_n\to f$ a.e., $\lvert f_n\rvert\le g\in L^1$ a.e. | $f\in L^1$, $\int f_n\to\int f$ |

$$
\text{MCT}\longrightarrow\text{Fatou's Lemma}\longrightarrow\text{DCT}
$$

The proof ideas to reconstruct are tail infima and $g\pm f_n$. DCT replaces monotonicity with integrable domination.
