# Today's Roadmap

## Proposition 2.1

Let $(X,\mathcal M)$ and $(Y,\mathcal N)$ be measurable spaces. Suppose that $\mathcal N$ is generated by a collection $\mathcal E$. Then a function

$$
f:X\to Y
$$

is $(\mathcal M,\mathcal N)$-measurable if and only if

$$
f^{-1}(E)\in\mathcal M
\qquad\text{for every }E\in\mathcal E.
$$

## Proposition 2.3

Let $(X,\mathcal M)$ be a measurable space and let

$$
f:X\to\mathbb R.
$$

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1. $f$ is $\mathcal M$-measurable.

2. For every $a\in\mathbb R$,

$$
f^{-1}((a,\infty))\in\mathcal M.
$$

3. For every $a\in\mathbb R$,

$$
f^{-1}([a,\infty))\in\mathcal M.
$$

4. For every $a\in\mathbb R$,

$$
f^{-1}((-\infty,a))\in\mathcal M.
$$

5. For every $a\in\mathbb R$,

$$
f^{-1}((-\infty,a])\in\mathcal M.
$$

## Proposition 2.13

Let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be nonnegative simple functions on a measure space $(X,\mathcal M,\mu)$, and let $E\in\mathcal M$.

The integral of a nonnegative simple function satisfies:

$$
\int_E c\phi\,d\mu
=
c\int_E\phi\,d\mu
\qquad(c\ge 0),
$$

$$
\int_E(\phi+\psi)\,d\mu
=
\int_E\phi\,d\mu+\int_E\psi\,d\mu,
$$

and if $\phi\le\psi$, then

$$
\int_E\phi\,d\mu
\le
\int_E\psi\,d\mu.
$$

Moreover, if $E_n\uparrow E$, then

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E_n}\phi\,d\mu
=
\int_E\phi\,d\mu.
$$

## Monotone Convergence Theorem

Let $(X,\mathcal M,\mu)$ be a measure space, and let

$$
\{f_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L^+
$$

satisfy

$$
f_n\le f_{n+1}
\qquad\text{for every }n.
$$

Define

$$
f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)
=
\sup_n f_n(x).
$$

Then $f\in L^+$ and

$$
\int_X f\,d\mu
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X f_n\,d\mu.
$$
